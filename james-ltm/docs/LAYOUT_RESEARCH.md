# Layout research: Greek house (oikos) and agora

Use these as the starting plans for districts and rooms. The owner asked for both. They suit a first-century Greek-speaking city such as Colossae or the cities James writes to.

## The Greek courtyard house (oikos)
Sources: [Houses and Households in Ancient Greece: Organization of Space](https://ecampusontario.pressbooks.pub/housesandhouseholdsancientgreece/chapter/organization-of-space/), [Olynthos Project: Olynthian houses](https://sites.lsa.umich.edu/olynthos-project/olynthian-houses/), [MIT: House of Many Colors, Olynthos](https://web.mit.edu/21h.405/www/olynthos/homc.html).

- **A single street entrance** leads into a **central open courtyard**. Every room opens off the courtyard through doorways, not open passages, which gave privacy.
- **Andron:** the formal dining and drinking room for male guests (symposium couches along the walls, often a decorated floor). A strong, distinctive room.
- **Kitchen and living room:** named on plans, but cooking and weaving often happened in the courtyard, because braziers and looms were portable.
- **Upper floor:** stairs to a second storey are attested at Olynthus.
- **Women's quarters (*gynaikonitis*):** mentioned in literary sources, but the archaeology doesn't clearly show a separate wing. Don't build a segregated wing as fact.

**LTM mapping:** stand in the courtyard with the entrance behind you. Rooms run clockwise from the room on the left of the entrance, for example: storeroom → kitchen → andron → living room → stairs and upper floor. The courtyard's own centre (an altar or well) can be a room too.

## The Greek agora
Sources: [Lake Forest College: Athenian Agora quick tour](https://campus.lakeforest.edu/academics/greece/AgoraTour.html), [Ancient Athens 3D: the Classical Agora](https://ancientathens3d.com/classical-agora/), [Athenian Agora plan](https://www.faculty.umb.edu/gary_zabel/Courses/Morals%20and%20Law/M+L/Plato/agorapln.htm).

- **A large open square** with a sacred centre: altars (such as the Altar of the Twelve Gods) and the monument of the Eponymous Heroes, where public notices were posted.
- **A processional road** (the Panathenaic Way) crosses the square.
- **Stoas** (long covered colonnades) line the edges and house shops, courts and meeting places: the Royal Stoa and Stoa of Zeus to the north-west, the South Stoa to the south, the Stoa of Attalos to the east.
- **Civic buildings** on the west side: the **bouleuterion** (council house) and the round **tholos** (office of the presiding councillors). A law court and water clock sit to the south-east.
- **A temple overlooks the square** from higher ground (the Hephaisteion).

**LTM mapping:** each stoa bay, shop or civic building is a room. Walk the perimeter clockwise from the entrance on the left (for example the gate stoa → shops → bouleuterion → tholos → court → fountain), and use the central altar or notice monument as a final room. Stoa colonnades give a natural, evenly spaced row of rooms.
