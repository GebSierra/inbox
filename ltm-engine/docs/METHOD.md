# The LTM memorization method (spec)

The owner's method for memorizing a book of the Bible with a location-triggered memory palace (LTM). **Every build must follow this exactly.** `tools/validate-book.mjs` and `tests/acceptance.mjs` enforce the parts that can be checked.

| # | Rule | Exact meaning | Memory-science basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Rote first** | Before placing a verse, the learner reads it aloud 10–20 times until they can say it roughly. Locations then fix and order what is already learned. | Repeated retrieval strengthens memory more than restudy alone (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006). The method of loci is strongest for *ordered* recall of material already encoded (Bower, 1970). |
| 2 | **One district per chapter** | Each chapter gets its own distinct area (market, temple, house…). No district holds two chapters. | Distinct contexts reduce interference between similar lists. Spatial chunking supports serial recall (Bower, 1970; Legge et al., 2012). |
| 3 | **Rooms of five verses** | A chapter of *n* verses has ceil(*n*/5) rooms. Room *k* holds verses 5*k*−4 … 5*k*; the last room may hold fewer. A room is any distinct place with a front, four corners and a centre that you can see while standing in front of it: an actual room, a gate court, a market stall, a stoa bay, a clearing on a hilltop. | Small, fixed-size chunks fit working-memory limits and give each room a predictable address (Cowan, 2001). |
| 4 | **Each verse on a spot** | Each verse is tied to one spot: a fixed corner or the centre of the room. A spot can be a feature of the place itself (a doorway, a column, a corner of a wall, a step) or an object standing there. Every spot should be easy to point at and easy to tell apart from the other spots in its room. **Every room has at least two objects**, and they should look natural to that place (a fountain in a court, baskets in a stall, a bench in a stoa). Now and then an object can be made to suit a particular verse, when it helps and still fits the place, but that is the exception, not the pattern. Never fill a room with objects that only exist to carry verses. Objects should be unique across the book where possible. | Loci must be distinctive and well separated to avoid confusion (Bower, 1970; Yates, 1966). |
| 5 | **Fixed order within a room** | As seen standing in front of the room: **1 near-left → 2 far-left → 3 far-right → 4 near-right → 5 centre.** Spot 5 is the centre of the square formed by spots 1–4, on an object if one stands there. A room with fewer than five verses uses spots 1, 2, 3… in that order and leaves the rest empty. | A constant traversal order is what turns the method of loci into a serial-recall tool (Bower, 1970; Dresler et al., 2017). |
| 6 | **Rooms follow one fixed route** | Rooms follow the natural route through the district, always in the same order. **In a town or court:** clockwise. Start at the room nearest the district entrance on the left, go up the left side, then come back down the right. **On a hillside:** a switchback trail from the highest point down, with the rooms at its turns, alternating sides, each lower than the last. Gates and wells can be rooms. | The same: one fixed route through the whole palace. |
| 7 | **Learners make their own vivid images** | Show at most one or two example images, phrased "for example, you could picture…". Images should be bigger than life, funny, moving and emotional. The place gives the spot; the learner makes the picture. | Self-generated, interactive, distinctive imagery is recalled better (Bower, 1970; McDaniel & Einstein, 1986). |
| 8 | **ESV text** | Verse text comes from `verses.json` (ESV, used with the owner's permission). The translation is fixed per book. | Verbatim recall needs one stable source text. |
| 9 | **Walk it back, and review** | After placing a room, the learner closes their eyes, walks the route and recites. They review the next day and again a week later. | Retrieval practice (Roediger & Karpicke, 2006) and spaced review (Cepeda et al., 2006). Method-of-loci training produces durable gains (Dresler et al., 2017). |

Virtual palaces work: building the palace in a virtual environment gives the same benefit as a real one (Legge et al., 2012). A meta-analysis supports the method of loci overall (Twomey & Kroneisen, 2021).

- 2026-11-12, owner: rule 4 changed from "each verse on an object" to "each verse on a spot". A spot can be a feature of the place or an object; every room has at least two objects, natural to the place; an object made for a verse is the exception. Rule 3 now says what counts as a room, rule 5 lets spot 5 be the bare centre in any room, rule 7 adds "the place gives the spot; the learner makes the picture". The validator now accepts place spots (`"place": true`) and checks for at least two objects per room; the critic rubric and reference prompts speak of spots.
