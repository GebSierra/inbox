# Plan: the Sermon on the Mount LTM (Matthew 5–7)

A memory palace for Matthew 5–7 on a hillside on the north-west shore of the Sea of Galilee, near Capernaum. It serves **two systems on one walk**:

- **The owner's method** (`ltm-engine/docs/METHOD.md`), which has priority in every design choice.
- **The outline system** (SOMoutline.pdf): the sermon's own sections, each tied to a natural break in the text. A **Layout** switch in the "i" panel hides room banners and markers and shows signposts for the outline sections. The places, spots and walk never change between modes.

Both systems run in verse order, so one walk serves both.

## 1. The whole palace

You **begin where he sat down** (5:1) and **walk down the mountain** (8:1):

| Chapter | Verses | Rooms | District | Ground | Landmark (seen from its open rooms) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | 48 | 10 | **The Summit**: the Crown and the Upper Meadow | open rock and grass on the Crown; worked land (quarry, hives, vines) in the Meadow | the stone watchtower |
| 6 | 34 | 7 | **The Farm** | one farmhouse round a central court on the upper terrace, its fields on the lower, inside a walled farmstead below the ridge's west end | the farmhouse's flat roof and its smoke |
| 7 | 29 | 6 | **The Shore Village** | the beach, the lakeside highway, the wadi mouth | the boats on the beach |

**Compass.** From this hill on the north-west shore the lake lies to the south, and **Hippos**, the town on its flat-topped hill across the lake to the south-east, is visible from the summit (5:14, "a city set on a hill").

**Site plan** (see `refs/plan/layout-map.png`, drawn on a bird's-eye view of the world; for chapter 6, `refs/plan/ch6-house-plan.png` supersedes it, and the old farm labels on layout-map.png no longer apply):
- **The Summit** is a ridge running east–west on the north side of the site, highest at its east end.
- **The Farm** lies on the gentle, nearly flat ground beyond the ridge's low west end, just below the Watchtower, between the ridge and the shore. Two terraces, marked by walls more than by steps in the ground: the farmhouse above, the fields below (§4).
- **The Shore Village** strings along the beach at the foot of the farm. A dry wadi comes down the slope beside it.
- The lake shore has been moved well south of the summit, so that the slope between them holds the whole farm and the village has its own flat strip of beach. The walk goes downhill all the way: summit, farm, shore.

**Routes (method rule 6).** The route takes the form that fits the ground:
- **On the hillside, the summit:** a switchback trail from the top down. Rooms stand at its turns, alternating sides, and each is lower than the one before. A place you naturally walk *through* (the Sheepfold's gate, the Ridge Road) stands on the trail itself instead of beside it; there the viewer stands on the trail facing along it.
- **In the farmhouse (chapter 6):** the court form, clockwise through the house, then out and down to the fields on the lower terrace (§4).
- **On the flat beach, the village:** a clockwise loop. You enter facing in, rooms run up your left side, cross at the far end, and come back down your right side.

The way from one district to the next leaves from the last room:
- The summit's last room is the Watchtower, at the low west end of the ridge. The path down to the farm's upper gate starts there.
- The farm's last room, the Olive Press, is beside its lower gate. The lane down to the beach starts there.
- The village ends beside its own entrance, at the wadi.

**Start.** The walk opens at the first room's own stop. You stand on the trail just below the crown, facing the Seat, with the olive tree on your left and the lake on your right. There is no landing anywhere else first.

## 2. Rules this plan follows

- **Rule 2:** one district per chapter.
- **Rule 3:** ceil(n/5) rooms per chapter. Room k holds verses 5k−4 … 5k, and spot i holds verse 5(k−1)+i.
- **Rule 4:** each verse is on a spot. A spot is either a feature of the place (**P**) or an object (**O**).
  - Every room has **at least two objects** that belong to the place.
  - Things chosen for a verse are rare: two in the whole book, marked **(v)**, each on the spot of its own verse and each natural to its place (the lantern post 5:15 and the oil flask 6:17; the storeroom of 6:6 is the room itself, and its spot 1 is the threshold, not the door). Where a place suits a passage as a whole (a storeroom, a granary field, a house on rock), the plan avoids putting a part on the very verse it would illustrate.
  - **No object or named feature repeats** (see the ledger in §7).
  - Animals and loose things never count towards the two objects.
  - The five spots in a room are all different kinds of thing.
- **Rule 5:** spots run 1 near-left → 2 far-left → 3 far-right → 4 near-right → 5 centre, as seen standing in front of the room. A shorter room uses 1, 2, 3… in that order.
- **Rule 6:** rooms follow one fixed route, in the form that fits the ground (§1): a switchback down the summit, a clockwise walk through the farmhouse, and a clockwise loop in the village.
- **Size:** an outdoor room is about 6 × 6 m, its spots on a square of about 5 m, and the viewer stands on the trail or street about 7 m from the centre. An indoor room (chapter 6's house) is about 10 × 10 m, and the viewer stands just inside its doorway, about 4 m from the centre.
- **Facing:** every room's front faces the path. The viewer stands on the path, looking at the room on the side the route gives it. Spot 1 is the corner nearest the viewer on their left (indoors: the corner left of the door). Rooms the trail runs *through* (the Sheepfold, the Ridge Road) are the exception to "front faces the path": the viewer stands on the trail facing along it, and the spots stand either side.
- **Rule 7:** the place gives the spot, and the learner makes the picture. Places may suit the passage as a whole (an inner storeroom for "go into your room"), but objects aren't chosen to illustrate single verses.
- **Rule 8:** ESV text.
- **Markers:** off by default.

## 3. Chapter 5: the Summit (10 rooms)

**Layout.** The top of the hill is a ridge running east–west. It is highest at its east end, where he sat, and falls steadily west. A dry-stone **field wall** crosses it and divides it in two:
- **The Crown** lies east of the wall and holds rooms 1–4 (outline: Setting, Beatitudes, Set Apart).
- **The Upper Meadow** lies west of the wall and holds rooms 5–10 (outline: But I Tell You).

**Why the Upper Meadow holds what it does.** It is one farming family's hill land, and its rooms are the work of that land, linked by low terrace lines and field paths: the quarry that supplies the terrace walls, charcoal for the farm's oven, bees among the wild thyme, the shepherds who graze the stubble, the road that takes the produce down, and the vineyard with its watchtower at the bottom of the hill.

**The field wall and the stile.** The wall must read as a real boundary, not a line dropped on the grass. It marks where the open hilltop ends and worked land begins:
- **Where it runs:** the full width of the ridge, from the brow of the north slope to the brow of the south slope, where the hillside drops away towards the lake, so there is no way round it.
- **What it's built of:** stones cleared from the Meadow's fields. It is knee to waist high, uneven, and ends at each side in a heap of clearance stones.
- **The ground changes at it.** The Crown side is open hilltop: grass, rock and wildflowers, with only a small cucumber patch and a threshing floor, and no terraces or ploughing. The Meadow side has the marks of work: low terrace lines across the slope, ploughed strips, and paths to the quarry and the hives.
- **The stile:** where the trail meets the wall, a gap is closed by a worn stone threshold slab you step over, with a single upright gatepost beside it. A tall cairn of clearance stones, topped with one white stone, marks the crossing, and the trail is worn bare as it passes through.

The outline's first big break (5:20 / 5:21) is the stile itself.

**Route** (rule 6, hillside form). A switchback trail runs down the ridge from the top. Rooms stand at its turns, alternating sides (north = the inland side, south = the lake side), and each is lower than the one before.
1. **The Seat** is on the very top. The walk opens here, at the Seat's own stop, facing it (§1, Start).
2. The trail runs straight on into **the Sheepfold**: the pen stands across the trail, which goes in by the fold's gateway and out by a narrow shepherd's gap in the far wall (owner, 2026-11-18). Then it turns down to the south for **the Lookout** on the lake edge, then to the north for **the Threshing Floor**. Its last leg goes down to the stile.
3. Over the stile, it keeps zigzagging west and down: south for **the Quarry**, north for **the Charcoal Burners' Clearing**, south for **the Bee Bank**, north for **the Shepherds' Camp**, then along **the Ridge Road**, which the trail itself becomes for that stretch: the room stands astride the road at the fork where the village track branches off (owner, 2026-11-18), and finally north for **the Watchtower** at the meadow's low west end.
4. The path down to chapter 6 leaves from the Watchtower. It runs west off the ridge's end to the farm's upper gate.

## 4. Chapter 6: the Farmhouse (7 rooms)

Redrawn 2026-11-18 at the owner's request: the farm becomes **one house you walk through**, not seven stations around a yard. Map: `refs/plan/ch6-house-plan.png` (north up).

**Layout.** The walled farmstead keeps its place, its two gates and its two terraces on the near-flat ground below the ridge's west end. The **upper terrace** holds a single-storey **four-room farmhouse** of fieldstone and plaster with a flat earth roof, about 32 m east–west by 24 m north–south (the house alone; the entry court lies north of it and the cistern court south): rooms on two sides (east and west) of an open central court, closed at the back by a wall with the back door (the "living room" of the owner's sketch, where the family works by day), an entry court before it and a small walled cistern court behind it. The rooms are lit from the open central court and from small windows. The **lower terrace** holds the fields and the olive press by the lower gate.

**Rule 6, court form.** Inside the house the walk runs **clockwise** as seen from above. You come in by the front door on the north side, facing south. The rooms go left (east), back (south), then right (west). Then you go out of the house and down the terrace. The rooms are closed rooms with doorways. Each is seen only from inside, from its viewer point, so no walls need cutting away. **Indoors the viewer stands just inside each room's doorway, its back to the door; spot 1 is the corner left of the door, and the spots run as rule 5 gives.** Interior rooms are about 10 × 10 m, so the viewer is about 4 m from the centre. The bird's-eye view hides the roofs over the house.

1. **6.1 the Entry Court**, outside the front door. You enter it through the upper gate.
2. Through the front door into the open central court (not used for verses). The first doorway on your **left** is **6.2 the Inner Storeroom**, its door standing open. It is a dead end, a room you go into and shut the door.
3. Back out to the central court; the next doorway along the left side opens into the back-left (south-east) room, **6.3 the Kitchen**.
4. Back into the central court and out by its back door into **6.4 the Cistern Court**, a small walled court behind the house, built from the old Spring's pieces (owner, 2026-11-18).
5. From the cistern court a doorway in the stall's south wall leads into the back-right (south-west) room, **6.5 the Ox Stall**. Its own side door on the **right-hand (west) side of the house** leads outside. (The front-right room is the sleeping room, closed, not used.)
6. Out of the stall's side door, along the west side, through the terrace gate (moved to the west end of the terrace wall) and down into **6.6 the Granary Field**.
7. Across the field to **6.7 the Olive Press** in the lower field by the lower gate. From there the lane goes on to the village.

The outline's big break, Focus Internally (6:1–18) / Focus Eternally (6:19–34), falls inside 6.4, the Cistern Court, between its third and fourth spots. The inside of the house holds the hidden life ("in secret"). The walk leaves the house at the stall's door, at 6:25 / 6:26, and the fields hold the birds of the air, the lilies of the field and "seek first the kingdom".

## 5. Chapter 7: the Shore Village (6 rooms)

**Layout.** An unwalled fishing village, like Capernaum, on the flat strip of beach at the foot of the farm, where the **lakeside highway** passes. The lane from the farm's lower gate reaches the beach at the village's east end. That is the entrance. Because the beach is flat, the village uses rule 6's clockwise form:
- Facing west along the shore, the **left side is the lakeside:** rooms 1–3, going west.
- The loop turns at the west end of the village.
- The **right side is the hillside:** rooms 4–6, coming back east.
- The **lakeside highway** is the village street itself: the packed-earth road along the shore that the whole loop walks, with the lakeside rooms below it and the hillside rooms above it. The Tax Table (7.3) stands on it at the west end.
- A dry **wadi** (a stream bed that floods in winter storms) starts in a gully below the farm's lower gate, runs down beside the lane on its west side and crosses the street just west of the entrance, before the first lakeside room. 7.5, the House on the Rock, stands on the rock shelf of its west bank. 7.6, the House on the Sand, stands in its sandy bed, beside the entrance.

The wadi gives the last pair of rooms their real setting: a house on the rock shelf of its bank, and a house in its sandy bed. Both are plain, standing houses. The learner pictures the storm. The crowd's reaction (7:28–29) closes the walk there.

## 6. Two modes on one walk

| | 5-verse rooms (default) | Outline |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Room banners ("The Seat 5:1–5") | shown | hidden |
| Spot markers and verse numbers | the "i" toggles (off by default) | hidden |
| Outline signposts (§3–5) | hidden | shown beside the spot of each section's first verse |
| Chapter cards on arrival | "Chapter 5 · The Summit" | the top-level section entered, e.g. "Set Apart · Matthew 5:13–20" |
| Prev / Next | room to room | signpost to signpost along the same path |
| Bird's-eye labels | the three districts | the top-level sections |

A break inside a room is shown by the signpost standing between two spots; the room is never split. The book file carries the outline as a list of sections in verse order: `outline: [{ path: ["Sanctified Focus", "Focus Internally", "Prayer", "Disciples' Prayer"], ref: "6:9–13", from: 9, at: "6.2.4" }]`. `path` holds every level, from the top down, so any depth works. A signpost's title is the levels that **begin** at its verse, joined with ›. For example, at 6:5 the sign reads "Prayer › Prayer Room". The chapter card uses `path[0]`.
